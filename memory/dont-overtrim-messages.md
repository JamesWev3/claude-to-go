When James asks for a message or draft to be shortened, trim it to a compact, nicely laid-out version that still carries a decent amount of information. Don't collapse it to a single line.

**Why:** On 2026-10-27 he asked for some messages to be shortened. My draft cut them to one line each, and he said it was too short and should be "a bit more pretty, and still gives you a decent amount of info".

**How to apply:** Aim for a few lines with some structure: a heading or label, the key facts, and maybe a small visual. Cut the padding, not the substance. Related: [[creative-diagrams]].

**Also (2026-11-10):** while guiding him through edits, terse replies (just diffs + "say typed") left him lost: "I have no idea what you're thinking or aiming to do." In working replies, keep the one-step-at-a-time pace but include: what I found and why it matters, the reason behind each edit (what the code is doing), and where the remaining plan is heading. Brevity rules cut padding, never the reasoning.

**Also (2026-11-12), shouted:** "WHY ARE YOUR REPLIES SO SMALL! YOU ARE LOSING YOUR MESSAGES INSIDE OF YOUR DIAGRAMS." I'd moved the real explanation into diagram tips (hidden until clicked) and left 2-3 lines of prose. Rule: every fact and reason must be in the visible prose. A diagram illustrates the text; it never replaces it, and its tips are bonus detail only. Cover what I found, why, what we agreed, what's next (but see the next note on length).

**Then (same day):** the full-prose rewrite was "too much text". The target is the middle: per point, 1-3 short prose lines that carry the fact + reason, paired with a diagram that shows it. Roughly 2 diagrams + a diff + ~10-15 lines of prose for a working reply. No headed essays, no tip-only reasoning.
